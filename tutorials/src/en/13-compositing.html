Compositing workflow

Compositing combines more than one image (or a painted layer) into a picture that feels coherent. The skill is less one magic tool than a sequence: select the subject, place it in a new document, transform it into perspective, refine the edge with a mask, then unify light and color.

A repeatable path

  1. Prepare the background and the subject as separate documents, or copy the subject onto a new layer in the background file.
  2. Build a rough outline with a selection or Object Selection, output a layer mask, then refine hair and glass in Select and Mask or on the mask itself.
  3. Free Transform to match size and the horizon; use Perspective or Warp when the subject must sit on a ground plane or a wall.
  4. Match the light: highlight and shadow direction on the subject must agree with the background. Curves or Hue/Saturation (preferably as masked adjustment layers) help.
  5. Add a shadow layer at the contact point (low opacity, Multiply or Soft Light). Without it the subject looks stuck on.
  6. Finish with a little shared grain or a gentle overall grade so pieces from different cameras feel like one capture.

In SimplePhoto

You can open several documents, copy and paste layers, and place or rasterize artwork (according to the current menus). Smart Objects suit pieces you will keep scaling. Layer groups can separate Background, Subject, Color, and Type. Toggle visibility for A/B checks, and avoid flattening the whole stack too early.

Photoshop-only notes

Generative Fill that “blends two pictures from a prompt,” and Firefly reference consistency, are Photoshop generative flows. SimplePhoto compositing relies on your selections, masks, transforms, and color work.
