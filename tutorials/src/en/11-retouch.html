Chapter 11 · Retouch

Retouch, heal, and clone

Retouching usually removes something small you do not want (dust, a blemish, a wire) or borrows texture from one place to another without leaving a seam. Work on a new layer or a copy, zoom in often, and match the surrounding light.

In SimplePhoto

  • Spot Healing Brush (J): tap a simple blemish.
  • Healing Brush (same J group): set a source, then paint the target so texture and tone blend.
  • Patch Tool: outline the area to replace and drag it onto clean texture.
  • Clone Stamp (S): copies source pixels literally — useful when structure must match (a railing, a brick joint). Pattern Stamp is in the same group.
  • The group also includes the Remove Tool, Content-Aware Move, and Red Eye. Dodge, Burn, and Sponge (O) adjust local lightness and saturation; Blur, Sharpen, and Smudge are available as well.

Healing Brush and Clone Stamp can sample from another open document. Change the source often so you do not stamp a repeating texture.

A sensible workflow

Remove the distractions that pull the eye first, then finer skin or dust work. If Spot Healing is enough, do not start with a large clone. When you finish, lower the layer opacity briefly to see whether you went too far. Be especially careful around eyes and silhouettes.

Photoshop-only notes

Generative Fill “remove this person and complete the background,” and some neural skin tools, are Photoshop cloud features. SimplePhoto’s Remove, Heal, and Clone tools are local; complex occlusions may need more manual work.
