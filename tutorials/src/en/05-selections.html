Selection tools

A selection tells the editor “change only this part.” Edges can be hard or feathered. After you select, brushes, adjustments, and filters apply inside that area (masks, in chapter 08, make this even more flexible).

Which tool to reach for

  • Rectangular / Elliptical Marquee (M): regular shapes; also useful when framing a poster or a hard-edged crop aid.
  • Lasso / Polygonal Lasso / Magnetic Lasso (L): freehand edges. Magnetic Lasso tries to cling to a contrasty outline.
  • Magic Wand (W): click regions of similar color; works well on flat backgrounds.
  • Quick Selection / Object Selection (same W group): paint over or frame a subject to separate a person or object from a background in broad strokes.

In SimplePhoto

These tools live in the left toolbar; a shared shortcut cycles the group. The Select menu has Select All, Deselect, Inverse, and Feather. Select and Mask refines hair and semi-transparent edges; you can output a selection, a layer mask, a new layer with a mask, or a new document. Object Selection also has New, Add, Subtract, and Intersect modes so you can refine the region in steps.

Habits for a cleaner selection

Get a rough selection first, then zoom in to tidy the edge. Add a little feather when you need a soft transition, or use the radius and contrast controls in Select and Mask. You rarely need to keep a marching-ants selection forever — turn it into a mask when you want to revise it later.

Photoshop-only notes

Some one-click cutouts and selection-plus-Generative Fill flows depend on Adobe’s cloud models. SimplePhoto offers local tools such as Object Selection, but do not expect the same results as Photoshop’s generative workspace.
